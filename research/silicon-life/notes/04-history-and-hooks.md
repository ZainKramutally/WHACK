Narrative arc that falls out of this: *a 130-year-old idea* (1891 to now), *popularised by fiction long before chemistry could test it* (Wells, Weinbaum, Asimov, Horta), *taken seriously by Cairns-Smith/NRC*, *poked at in the lab (Arnold 2016)*, and *finally reviewed systematically in 2020: the honest answer is "mostly no, in water; maybe in sulfuric acid"*. That is the honest framing for the pitch: the sim lets you try what nature may have rejected.

## 3. Candidate 15-second hooks (format: provocative fact then door)

Each is ~35-45 spoken words max. Facts are tagged to section 2.

1. **The flip** (strongest). "The ground under your feet is 28% silicon and 0.02% carbon. You are the opposite: 18% carbon, almost no silicon. About 1,400 to 1 against, and life picked carbon anyway. Today, you get to pick differently." (crust/body facts)
2. **Pocket.** "Hold up your phone. That's sand we taught to think. Nobody ever asked what happens if sand learns to eat. So we built the world where it does." (silicon chips from sand; `[POP]` + `[INFERRED]`)
3. **Horta.** "In 1967 Captain Kirk met a living rock and nearly killed it, because he assumed life had to look like him. We built a world where you decide what 'life' is made of, and watch what evolves." (Horta, 9 Mar 1967)
4. **Sagan.** "Carl Sagan called himself a carbon chauvinist. This world has no chauvinists. You choose the element, we run a million years." (Sagan 1973 quote via Petkowski)
5. **Nature already cheats.** "About a fifth of all the photosynthesis on Earth is done by creatures in glass shells: diatoms. Life on Earth already builds with sand. What if it went all the way?" (diatom ~20% of primary production; phrased to avoid the O2 claim)
6. **Venus.** "Fifty kilometres above Venus the air is room temperature, and the clouds are sulfuric acid. A 2020 study found silicon chemistry works *better* there than in water. So: what grows in acid clouds?" (Petkowski 2020; Venus cloud layer)
7. **Lab proof.** "In 2016 a Nobel winner's lab trained bacteria to make a silicon-carbon bond that nature never invented. That was one bond. We're asking what happens with a whole world full of them." (Kan et al. 2016; Arnold Nobel 2018)
8. **Cautionary / honest.** "Scientists have asked this question for 130 years. The honest answer is 'probably not in water'. So we built a place where you can test it anywhere else." (timeline; Petkowski 2020 conclusion)

Ranking for a no-tech-background judge: 1, 2, 3 (all land in under 10 seconds, all have a "whoa" fact and a verb: "you choose").

Delivery note: if using #1 or #2, open **cold**, with the sim already running in the background (docs/TRACK.md creative cold open), then say the line. Avoid "Hi, we're team...".

## 5. Pitch-use cautions

- Do not say "scientists believe silicon life exists / is possible" without qualification; the 2020 systematic review says silicon-only life is "almost certainly impossible" in water-based environments. Say: "scientists have asked this for 130 years; we built a sandbox to explore it".
- Do not quote Wells verbatim until section 0 is resolved.
- If the sim has a "Horta" creature, call it homage, not a copy; keep any visual away from the Paramount costume design.
- The arsenic story is a good one-liner for "yes, strange-chemistry life claims have been wrong before; that is why the sim is a what-if".
